## §1 — The register: `Yahwe`, `Erohimu`, and `c`

Three facts about standard Gĩkũyũ set the Name table
(`docs/methodology/translation-discipline/kik.md`, written in Gĩkũyũ).

**Gĩkũyũ has no `l`.** The orthography writes `r`. So `אלהים` is
**`Erohimu`**, `אל` is **`Ere`**, `אלוה` is **`Eroha`**.

**A Gĩkũyũ word ends in a vowel.** So `יהוה` is **`Yahwe`**, not *Yahweh*;
`צבאות` with the Name is **`Yahwe Cebaoti`**, nothing between.

**`ש` and `ס` are written `c`**, Gĩkũyũ's own letter for that sound:
`שדי` **`Cadai`**, `שבת` **`Cabati`**, `שאול` **`Ceori`**, `משה` **`Moce`**,
`ישראל` **`Yiceraeri`**.

Where the Name is joined to a prefix, **`Yahwe` itself does not change** — the
prefix attaches to the whole word.

## §2 — `Ngai` keeps a lawful place, in lower case

`Ngai` is the ordinary Gĩkũyũ word for God and the first refusal in the
discipline: **a good word, but not the Name.** Refused in the Name's place with
it: `Mwathani`, `Mwenenyaga`, `Mũrungu`, `Jehova`, and the Swahili `Mungu`,
`Bwana`.

But **`ngai` in lower case is right** where the text means the gods of the
nations. Exodus 32:8 `אלהיך` — *these are your gods, Israel* — reads
**`ngai ciaku`** in the token row. *Open:* the flow of the same verse reads
`Ngai cia ku`, capitalised and split. The row is the lawful form; the flow
should agree with it.

## §3 — A human lord is `mwathi`

`אדון`, and `אדני` addressed to a man, is **`mwathi`**. Genesis 44:19 —
Judah to Joseph, `אדני שאל את עבדיו` — reads **`Mwathi wakwa`**. Not an erasure
of the Name; the Hebrew says *master*. `אדני` addressed to God is
**`Adonai`**.

## §4 — Genesis 1:1

```
Kĩambĩrĩria-inĩ Erohimu nĩombire ⟨את⟩ igũrũ na ⟨את⟩ thĩ.
```

The marker twice, the Name register held, `nĩombire` for `ברא`.

## §5 — Three faults from the first verses, written into the discipline

Read in Genesis on 2026-10-01, and added to `kik.md` within the hour as rows
of right / refused / why.

- **Acute accents for the tilde letters.** `aría` for **`arĩa`**, `Íthĩranĩ`
  for **`Ĩthĩranĩ`**, `kŵĩrĩ` for **`kwĩrĩ`**. Gĩkũyũ marks two vowels, `ĩ`
  and `ũ`, and no others.
- **`l` kept in names.** `Abimeleki`, `Metucelahi`, `Yicimaeli`,
  `Oholibama` — the names are exactly where `l → r` applies:
  **`Abimereki`**, **`Metucerahi`**, **`Yicimaeri`**, **`Ohoribama`**.
- **Swahili words.** `kilima` (Swahili *hill*) for **`kĩrĩma`**;
  `amalaaika`, `ila`.

The second of these measurably took (see `PROVENANCE.md`). The first and third
did not fully: two words held on after they were named.

## §6 — The stubborn telling: two words, and their neighbours

What this rendering leans toward when it slips: **Swahili.** `aría` and
`kilima` kept coming back after the discipline named both. Later readings added
`mfalme` (Swahili *king*), `milango` (Swahili *gates*) and `nyinyi` (Swahili
*you*, plural — 1 Samuel 8:18, the token row for `לכם`). These are words, not a
systematic class; they want a one-to-one word correction across the corpus, and
a Gĩkũyũ ear to confirm each replacement. **Not yet applied.**

## §7 — The Name, where it is not `Yahwe`

The Name has held almost everywhere it stands. The known exceptions:

- **1 Samuel 8:18** — `ולא יענה יהוה אתכם`: the token row has `יהוה` →
  **`Nyerere`**, and the flow follows it. A personal name stood in the Name's
  place. *Open — to be re-rendered.*
- **2 Samuel 18:28** — the flow reads *Yehova, Erohimu waku*. *Open.*
- Further places where the Name came out as `Erohimu`. *Open.*

## §8 — The Hebrew surface restored

After the first pass, some token rows carried letters that are not the Hebrew
record's own inside the `surface` field — Latin, Cyrillic, and a replacement
character. Each was restored from the source text **by token position**, which
is arithmetic, not judgment, and re-checked to zero. In
`1-chronicles/28/14` one token had come back nested — its `surface` held a whole
token — and was un-nested. 2026-10-02.

## §9 — English left inside the brackets

`⟨…⟩` is for words Gĩkũyũ grammar requires that the Hebrew did not write.
Some verses carried **English** there instead (`⟨the⟩`). The earlier checks
stripped the brackets before testing for English, so these went unseen at
first. Lawful Gĩkũyũ supplied words in brackets — Deuteronomy 6:4's `⟨nĩ⟩` —
are not this fault. Affected verses were removed for re-rendering. 2026-10-06.

## §10 — Verses removed for re-rendering

On 2026-10-06 every verse whose token row did not match the Hebrew token count,
or was empty, or carried English inside the brackets, was **removed** from this
repository, so that what remains can be read with trust. Those verses, and the
ones never rendered in the first pass, are being rendered again now and will be
re-checked against the same discipline. Until that lands, a missing verse file
means *being re-rendered*, not *skipped*.

## §11 — Open: what a Gĩkũyũ ear should read first

Noticed while preparing these documents, by reading the four verses quoted in
`README.md` and their neighbours. None has been changed.

- **Genesis 1:26** — `ובעוף השמים` → `na ciugo cia igũrũ`. `ciugo` ordinarily
  means *words*; the Hebrew is *birds*. And `בדגת הים` → `nguo cia nyamũ cia
  iria`, where the Hebrew is *fish of the sea*.
- **Psalm 23:1** — the token row gives `לא` → `nĩti` and `אחסר` →
  `ndĩkahotana kũgima`, but the flow joins them as `nĩndĩkahotana kũgima`.
  Whether the flow still carries the negation of `לא` — *I shall not lack* —
  is a question for a Gĩkũyũ reader.
- **Isaiah 53:5** — `מוסר` (*chastisement, discipline*) is glossed `ihĩtia`,
  the singular of the very word that renders `מפשענו` (*our transgressions*,
  `mahĩtia maitũ`) earlier in the verse. Two different Hebrew roots under one
  Gĩkũyũ noun. Also `rĩa thayũ yaitũ`: the noun-class agreement wants a
  Gĩkũyũ ear.
- **Numbers 24:3** — `נאם בלעם` reads `kiugo kya Bireamu`; the discipline
  writes `kĩa`. Spelling variant or slip.
- **Genesis 29:16** — `לבן` reads `Lebanu`: `l` kept in a name (§5).

## §12 — Five spellings left for a Gĩkũyũ ear

The discipline names five choices the machine did not settle, and asks a
Gĩkũyũ reader to decide them:

1. **`c` for `ש` / `ס`** (Cadai · Cabati · Ceori · Moce · Yiceraeri) — or
   *sh* / *s*?
2. **Final vowels on names** (Erohimu · Abarahamu · Dawidi) — or a consonant
   ending?
3. **`mũrathi`** (Gĩkũyũ's own word) for `נביא` — or *mũnabii*?
4. **`cedeki`** for `צדק` — Gĩkũyũ writes `d` only after `n`.
5. **`Ere`** for `אל` — or *Eri*?

## §13 — The interface catalog

The Gĩkũyũ interface strings for the Selah reader were also machine-rendered.
Two words were flagged there for a Gĩkũyũ ear (`fungũra`, `kĩportuguese`), and
a second attempt at the strings that came back English was **reverted whole** —
it returned words that could be checked and were wrong (a word close to
`thayũ` *peace* for the letter-skip, *day* for direction, *holy* for a section
heading). Those strings stay English until someone who reads Gĩkũyũ fills them.
